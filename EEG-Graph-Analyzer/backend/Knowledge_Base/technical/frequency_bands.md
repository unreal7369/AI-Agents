# EEG Frequency Bands

## Purpose

This document provides terminology for describing EEG activity by frequency band.

Frequency-band terminology should be used as a descriptive feature. A frequency label by itself does not establish a diagnosis, seizure, epileptiform abnormality, or localization.

## Common Frequency Bands

### Delta

Delta refers to very slow EEG activity, conventionally approximately 0.5–4 Hz.

When describing an EEG image, delta activity may be described as slow activity when the displayed time scale supports the frequency assessment.

### Theta

Theta refers approximately to 4–8 Hz activity.

Theta activity may occur physiologically in appropriate states and ages and may also occur as part of abnormal slowing depending on the clinical context and distribution.

### Alpha

Alpha refers approximately to 8–13 Hz activity.

A posterior dominant rhythm in the alpha range may be described when the waveform, distribution, state, and available technical information support that description.

### Beta

Beta refers approximately to 13–30 Hz activity.

Beta activity is relatively fast activity and should be described according to the visible waveform and available technical information.

### Gamma

Gamma generally refers to activity above approximately 30 Hz.

A static EEG image may not provide sufficient information to reliably characterize high-frequency activity, particularly when display resolution, filtering, sampling information, or time scale is unavailable.

## Estimating Frequency From an EEG Image

Frequency should not be assigned from waveform appearance alone.

Reliable frequency estimation from an EEG image requires an interpretable horizontal time scale or equivalent timing information.

If the time scale is unavailable or unclear:

- Do not report an exact frequency in Hz.
- Do not assign a precise frequency band solely from visual appearance.
- Use qualitative terminology such as "slow activity", "mixed-frequency activity", or "faster activity" only when supported by the visible waveform.

## Frequency Versus Pattern

Frequency band and EEG pattern are different concepts.

For example:

- Delta describes a frequency range.
- Theta describes a frequency range.
- A periodic discharge describes a temporal pattern.
- A rhythmic discharge describes a temporal pattern.
- A spike or sharp wave describes waveform morphology.

A waveform may have both a frequency characteristic and a pattern classification. One should not be substituted for the other.

## Image-Only Limitation

When interpreting a screenshot or photograph of an EEG:

- Report only frequency information supported by the displayed time scale.
- Do not infer exact Hz when timing information is absent.
- Do not infer an EEG diagnosis from frequency alone.
- Do not infer seizure activity from fast, slow, rhythmic, or periodic appearance alone.

## Reporting Language

When frequency can be reasonably assessed:

"Background activity includes predominantly [frequency description] activity."

When exact frequency cannot be established:

"Mixed-frequency activity is visible; precise frequency characterization is limited by the available image and/or time scale."

When slowing is visible but exact frequency is uncertain:

"Slow activity is present in the displayed tracing; precise frequency characterization is limited by the available image."

## Evidence Constraint

Frequency descriptions must remain consistent with the information actually visible in the EEG image.

The RAG system should provide terminology and reporting guidance. It must not manufacture frequency values, calibration, or timing information that are not present in the image.
